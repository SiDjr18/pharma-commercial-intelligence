08 · Methodology & Data Quality

How every number is produced

Market definition, data grain, periods, metrics, units, scoring and scenario methods, limitations and data-quality controls — the basis for trusting (and questioning) what this product shows.

Data foundation

This build runs on the SYNTHETIC dataset. Entities, codes and numbers are fictional and structure-only (generated by pci_synthetic; no value copied or derived from the licensed data). The description below is the private source whose schema it reproduces.
  • Source: a private, licensed IMS-style national secondary-sales audit (one workbook sheet, 105,317 packs × 203 columns). The workbook is never read or exposed by this application.
  • Grain: one row per pack; monthly value, units and counting-unit quantity. The finest grain this application shows is product within a therapy subgroup — already an aggregate.
  • Coverage: national level only. There is no geography, channel, prescriber or promotion data.
  • Analytical layer: pack-level Parquet + DuckDB views, independently re-implemented in Python and cross-validated field by field.

Market definition

The primary market is the therapy subgroup (an analytical definition from the source therapy hierarchy). Other levels: therapy area (supergroup), therapy group, molecule and the total market. A product can sell in several subgroups, so market membership is decided at pack level.

Identity: products are keyed by product code; brand names are labels and are not unique across companies. Companies are the source company field. Opportunity scoring uses the product-in-subgroup key.

Time periods

Three bases, each compared with the same window twelve months earlier: Month; Calendar YTD (January to the selected month — not the Indian financial year); MAT (the twelve months to the selected month). The windows for the current selection, as returned by the engine:

Growth needs a complete comparison window: Month growth from June 2022, YTD growth from January 2023, MAT growth from May 2023. Earlier periods show growth as “n/a”, never 0%.

Opportunity methodology

A descriptive score from 0 to 100: each component is converted to a percentile (mid-rank) within the national reference population, then weighted. It ranks observed evidence; it is not a forecast, probability or guarantee. Entities below the evidence thresholds are not scored and are never shown as zero.

Scenario methodology

Five deterministic what-if types on an observed baseline: price change, volume change, price + volume, market growth (market entities) and target market share (product or company within a market). Price is value ÷ units (₹ per pack). Every output is labelled Observed Assumed or Calculated. There is no elasticity, forecasting, competitor response or seasonality; out-of-range assumptions are rejected, not corrected.

Unsupported analysis (by design)

  • Geography (state, region, zone, city) — the source is national only.
  • Channel and SSA/HSA/DSA splits — their meaning is unconfirmed, so they are not used.
  • Prescriber / HCP, promotion, forecasting, price elasticity — not in the data and not modelled.

Requests for these return an explicit “unsupported” state rather than an approximation.

Data quality controls

  • Every numeric source column is reconciled to the analytical layer; the source's own MAT, YTD and price fields are reproduced.
  • SQL and an independent Python engine agree field by field; fault-injection tests guard every engine.
  • Opportunity scores are pinned by a methodology fingerprint; any methodology change requires a new version.
  • Scenario arithmetic is property-tested (zero change reproduces the baseline).
  • The AI Analyst's answers are checked by deterministic QA: every number must trace to a tool result field.
Units and counting-unit scale (’000) are inferred from workbook labels; value in ₹ crore is confirmed. These notes appear as caveats on every result that uses them.
